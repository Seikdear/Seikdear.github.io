excerpt: "Ofcial crime statistics systematically understate the true incidence of criminal activity, a measurement problem with particular consequences for how researchers and policymakers interpret spatial variation in crime, its relationship to local housing markets, and disparities between urban and
rural areas. We develop a semiparametric spatial stochastic frontier model that recovers latent crime
incidence from reported crime data while jointly accounting for under-reporting and spatial spillovers
across neighboring counties. The framework allows both covariate eﬀects and spatial spillovers to
vary ﬂexibly with local housing-market conditions and is estimated using a computationally tractable
two-step generalized method of moments procedure. We apply the model to U.S. county-level Uniform Crime Reporting data for 2014–2022 and address the simultaneity between police presence and
reported crime through an instrumental-variables strategy based on police-related government expenditure. We fnd a nonlinear, U-shaped relationship between spatial spillovers in reported crime
and the standardized local house price index for fve of six crime categories, consistent with enforcement capacity and public scrutiny varying nonlinearly with neighborhood wealth; this pattern
is absent for public-order crimes, which are largely enforcement-discretionary rather than victimreported. Estimated under-reporting is largest for property crime and smallest for rape, sex, and
vice crimes, and is consistently more pronounced in urban than in rural counties, a gap explained
mainly by diﬀerences in police presence rather than educational attainment. These fndings indicate
that relying solely on recorded crime can substantially misrepresent underlying public-safety needs.
By explicitly accounting for reporting frictions and spatial interactions, the proposed framework provides a decision-support tool for improving policing strategies, targeting crime-prevention programs,
allocating administrative resources, and coordinating interventions across neighboring jurisdictions."
