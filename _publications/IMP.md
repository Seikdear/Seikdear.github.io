excerpt: "I construct the first Divisia monetary aggregates and corresponding dual user cost indexes for Bangladesh using monthly data from 2005 up to the present. Then, I investigate various structural vector autoregression models (SVARs) using a block triangular specification advanced by Keating (2019), nonrecursive identification, and time-varying specification to reassess how monetary disturbances should be measured in an economy characterized by financial frictions, directed credit programs, and changes in the monetary policy framework. The investigation of the Bangladesh economy reveals that the policy interest rate, as a monetary policy indicator, exhibits the price puzzle, even when the model's information set is expanded to include the energy price index or the federal funds rate. In contrast, Divisia M2, as an indicator of monetary policy, provides an improved sensible response for prices and output compared to the policy rate as the indicator. Importantly, the Divisia M2 can resolve the price puzzle even without including the additional information sets."
